FENRIR — THE TERRAIN ENGINE
===========================

The changelog, at the user's request: what each change did and why, in the
order they were made. FENRIR (Fusion-Enabled Networked Real-time
Interactive Rendering) began as MEWD, a Doom-style engine (Made Entirely
Without Doom), became a Godot 4 game on golf's procedural islands, and is
now the islands' terrain engine alone: a place — a real one out of open map
data, or golf's — and a camera to fly round it. The game's own entries, up
to 0000035, are in the git history (this file before 0000039); what
follows starts where the real places did.

README.txt says how to run it, its keys and its command line.


A REAL PLACE ON EARTH: OESCHINENSEE (0000036)

At the user's request ("i want a system that will take coordinates on
earth and within defined boundaries (a square) render terrain faithfully
using the chunk based terrain system in mewd-engine, using open source
map data to define height water level and vegetation / biomes. i want
this to use high res pbr textures on the map, but still use billboard
vegetation, albeit high res"):

  THE IDEA. golf's island system asks one object, the field, every
  question it has: how high the ground is, where the coast is, what the
  ground is made of, how thick the wood. EarthField
  (godot/island/scripts/world/SCRIPT_earth_field.gd) is an IslandField
  that answers them out of grids made from open map data instead of out
  of noise. So the chunk mesher, its LODs, the terrain and plant bakes,
  the scatters and the crowd's ground all build a real place the way
  they build golf's islands, unchanged: a square cut out of the world
  with a cliff all round into the cloud.

  THE DATA (tools/earth_fetch.py, run once, off the game; Python with
  numpy and Pillow, everything fetched over plain HTTPS with no key, and
  cached in tools/.earth-cache/):
    - HEIGHT: the AWS Terrain Tiles (Mapzen's Terrarium PNGs: SRTM,
      GMTED, ETOPO1 and the national lidar sets merged), zoom 14,
      bicubic onto a 2 m grid. --dem copernicus reads Copernicus GLO-30
      out of its cloud-optimised GeoTIFFs instead (a surface model: the
      canopy is in it). The tiles under Switzerland are 30 m data
      brought up to 6 m and show it as a 30 m checker on every slope, so
      the height is blurred 8 m (--smooth) and the field puts relief
      finer than that back by noise, more on rock than on turf.
    - WHAT IS ON THE GROUND: ESA WorldCover 10 m (2021), its eleven
      classes (trees, shrubs, grass, crops, built-up, bare, snow and
      ice, water, wetland, mangrove, moss) read tile by tile out of its
      cloud-optimised GeoTIFFs by range request. Each class is sampled
      as a soft share, at points pushed about a few metres by noise, so
      a wood's edge wanders instead of stepping in 10 m pixels.
    - OPENSTREETMAP through the Overpass API (any of several servers;
      Switzerland's own for a square inside it): the lakes' and rivers'
      shorelines, the rivers and streams, the roads, tracks and paths,
      the railways, the buildings, the forests' leaf type, and the rock,
      scree, sand, wetland and glacier areas, all drawn onto the 2 m
      grid. With no server answering it goes on without them.
  From those it works out, on one square grid x east and z south:
    - THE WATER. Every body WorldCover or OSM has (OSM's shoreline where
      it has one), found as connected areas, and each given its level:
      a lake flat at the ground's own height across it (the DEMs flatten
      a lake to its surface), the sea at 0, a river falling with its
      banks (their heights relaxed across it). Its bed is carved under
      it by how far from the shore (a lake down to a fifth of its
      half-width, at most 35 m; a river a few metres) wherever the DEM
      is only the flat top of the water, and the banks are held up out
      of it, so the water's edge is where the ground meets its level.
      Streams under 4 m wide are pebbled channels sunk 0.6 m, not
      slivers of water; so is a lake's arm that climbs away from it (an
      outflow the maps drew joined to the lake: its ground stands metres
      over the lake's level, and given that level it was cut into a
      trench 30 m deep), and a small tarn the DEM never flattened takes
      its own middle height for its level.
    - TWELVE SURFACE LAYERS: grass, forest floor, rock, scree, sand,
      soil, snow, mud, paving, dry scrub, moss and the bed under the
      water — WorldCover's classes as layers, sharpened by OSM's rock,
      scree, sand, wetland and glacier areas (their straight sides worn
      into wandering outlines), rock wherever the ground is steeper than
      36 to 50 degrees (62 under snow), scree on the steep turf, a beach
      on the sea's shore and pebbles round a lake's, the roads (asphalt
      or earth by their surface), the railways' ballast and the
      buildings' footprints on top.
    - THE PLANTS: trees off WorldCover's tree cover (none on the roads,
      the rock, the snow or the water, thinning on the steep), the
      conifer share of them off OSM's leaf type where it is mapped and
      otherwise the climate's guess (firs coming in over the broadleaves
      from 1500 m less 25 m a degree of latitude past 20, all firs 700 m
      above that, and north of 52 degrees), bushes off the shrubland and
      the woods' edges, grass off the turf.
  And writes godot/island/earth/<name>/: height.grid (2 m, 16-bit),
  water.grid and wkind.grid (4 m: the surface, and sea, lake or river),
  cover.grid (4 m: the twelve layers as three RGBA pictures),
  veg.grid (4 m), region.json (how to read them, the water bodies, the
  sources and the attribution their licences ask for, and a digest),
  and pictures of each in preview/ for people. --register also writes
  the field and the scene (copies of OESCHINENSEE's) and the Islands
  entry. A 3 km square is about 5 MB and takes under a minute.

  THE FIELD reads them once per process (the mesher's and the scatters'
  per-thread clones share them) and answers:
    - base_height: the 2 m grid, bilinear, less the datum (a metre under
      the region's lowest ground, so the island's y = 0 is under all of
      it), plus the fine relief. No shore ramp: the land runs to the
      square's edge.
    - coast_y, a new hook on IslandField the mesher now asks for the
      height of the coastline (the coast's vertices and the cliff's lip
      start there): on golf's islands it is the shore level, as before;
      on a real place it is the ground at the edge, so the square ends
      in a cut face falling into the cloud instead of a ramp down to a
      shore that is not there.
    - sample() carries four more keys: "veg" (tree, conifer, bush, fern
      and grass densities), "water" (how deep over the ground), "water_y"
      and "cover" (the twelve layers); its "weights" are the twelve
      folded into golf's four, for the code that reads those.
    - height_at, the crowd's ground: none where the water is deeper than
      0.9 m (`wade_depth`), so a lake is a coast to the crowd.
    - the bakes: `data_digest` (the region's digest and this script's
      own) is a stored property, so the terrain, plant and ground bakes
      key themselves on the data as well as the settings.
  It is square_land with no coast wobble, one hub the region's size, no
  satellites, every golf feature off and no rounded lip on the cliff.
  Its chunks' skirts are 10 m deep and shaded with the ground's own
  normal (`chunk_skirt_shaded`, a new IslandField switch, off on golf's
  islands): on a mountain a coarse chunk beside a fine one leaves a step,
  and a skirt lit as a wall drew every LOD seam as a dark line across
  the slope.

  THE LOOK:
    - THE GROUND (SHADER_terrain_earth.gdshader) blends the twelve
      layers off the cover grid's three pictures (handed to the material
      by SCRIPT_earth_dress.gd when the island is made), at the data's
      own 4 m whatever the chunk's LOD; the mesher's vertex colours are
      not read. Each layer is a CC0 photo-scanned material at 2048 px
      (Poly Haven and ambientCG; tools/earth_pack.py fetches them; three
      of them tinted in the material to sit together: the turf duller,
      the forest floor darker, the slate rock lighter):
      colour and height in one 12-layer texture array, normal, roughness
      and occlusion in another. Only the layers with weight are sampled;
      where two meet the taller texels win (height blending); steep
      ground is projected from the side too; a second sample at a
      quarter of the frequency further off and a broad variation in
      brightness break the tiling. Lit by the island's own light
      unbanded, on the normal-mapped normal, with the occlusion on the
      ambient and the sun's highlight off the roughness, then the
      island's fog. The cliff all round wears the same rock.
    - THE WATER (SHADER_water_earth.gdshader): flat sheets of the 4 m
      water grid at each body's level, laid over the carved beds by
      SCRIPT_earth_dress.gd, with how deep it is and a river's flow in
      the vertices. The sky reflected by Fresnel off two drifting wave
      maps (a tiling one made by earth_pack, by FFT), the sun's glint,
      its own colour through it by depth, foam where it thins over the
      shore, a river's maps running downstream. Where water reaches the
      square's edge it is closed by a wall of water down to the bed.
    - THE PLANTS stay billboards, at the user's request, but high res:
      CC0 Poly Haven models (a full-grown fir and fir and pine saplings
      for the trees, three broadleaves, young firs and four shrubs for
      the bushes, ferns, nettles and celandine for the undergrowth, two
      grasses and dandelions for the tufts: 34 sprites in all; four
      models that would not draw one plant at a time, or drew black,
      are left out, earth_pack.py says which) rendered side-on by Godot itself
      (tools/earth_billboards.gd, through earth_pack.py billboards),
      each plant of a many-plant model drawn on its own, lit from the
      front and above with its own shadows so the crown has volume,
      at twice the size and brought down to 1024 x 1024 (broadleaves),
      512 x 1024 (firs), 512 x 512 and 512 x 256, with a burn map
      beside each for the billboard shader's fire. Their materials read
      them texel for texel (lofi_texels_per_m 0).
    - VegScatter now takes `broadleaf_sprites` beside the firs (and
      `broadleaf_height`): a tree is one or the other by the field's
      conifer share where it gives one, `broadleaf_share` where not.
      Where the sample has "veg", the class densities are the scene's
      forest densities times the field's own (trees, bushes, the
      undergrowth), and GrassScatter's tufts likewise.
    - The sky is EnviroSky's (A REAL PLACE'S SKY, below): the sun and
      the moon where they really are over the place, clouds and weather.
      The air and fog are a pale blue, thinner and further off than golf's
      islands so a valley's far side shows; the Environment's own fog is
      off (seen from a peak it whitened the whole valley), the ground and
      the water fog themselves. The Islands entry has "palette": false, so the
      photographs keep their colour (the pixel grid stays the picture
      settings').

  OESCHINENSEE is the first: a 3 km square of the Bernese Oberland
  round the lake under the Bluemlisalp (46.4986 N, 7.7270 E). 1527 to
  2567 m; the lake 1.18 square km at the DEM's 1569.5 m (the map says
  1578; SRTM's datum and its flattening of the water put it there),
  seven smaller tarns, the larch and spruce on the west shore, the
  pastures, the scree fans and the cliffs. You are put down on the
  pasture by the west shore (Islands "start", a new key: metres east
  and south of the middle, since a real place's middle may be its
  lake), and the ground search for the crowd and the start now widens
  until it finds land rather than giving up on a point in the water.

  A NEW PLACE:
    python3 tools/earth_fetch.py NAME --lat .. --lon .. --size METRES \
        --title "TITLE" --register
    godot --headless --editor --quit
    godot --headless --script res://tools/bake_island.gd -- --map=earth_NAME
  The earth pack (the textures and the sprites) is shared by every
  region and only needs making again to change it:
    python3 tools/earth_pack.py textures      (--res 1k for a lighter one)
    python3 tools/earth_pack.py models
    python3 tools/earth_pack.py billboards    (Godot under xvfb-run)
    python3 tools/earth/make_scene.py         (OESCHINENSEE's scene)

  godot/tests/earth_test.gd checks the field against its own data (the
  ground to the sample, the square, the edge's height, the lake, deep
  water no ground, the layers summing to one, the woods and their firs,
  the bake digest) and then the level through the game.

  The island field and the mesher changed (coast_y), so every island
  bakes again; the builds do that themselves. The exports now carry
  *.grid files.


A REAL PLACE'S SKY: CLOUDS, THE CLOCK AND THE WEATHER, AFTER ENVIRO

At the user's request ("make a cloud sky etc system reminiscent of the
enviro 3 unity plugin"): EnviroSky (godot/island/scripts/
SCRIPT_enviro_sky.gd), one node in a real place's scene in place of
golf's sky cycle, and the sky shader it drives (godot/island/shaders/
SHADER_sky_enviro.gdshader). OESCHINENSEE has it.

  THE AIR: single scattering through a spherical atmosphere, Rayleigh
  and Mie (Nishita's model, as wwwtyro's glsl-atmosphere has it), from
  the island's real height over the sea: the blue overhead, the pale
  horizon, the reds of a sunset and the twilight after it are the
  physics', not a gradient's. Below the horizon the air goes on as at
  the horizon (the island floats over the void). A weather's haze
  thickens the low sky.

  THE CLOUDS: a layer of cumulus raymarched through a shell of the sky
  on the planet's curve, so it falls away to the horizon, its base a
  weather's `base` metres over the place's highest ground and its top
  `thickness` over that. Shaped by a 128-cube Perlin-Worley noise
  (billows) eroded by a 32-cube Worley detail (wisps), spread over the
  sky by a weather map whose threshold is the coverage (read off the
  map's own percentiles, so 0.45 covers about 45 per cent), stratus to
  towering cumulus by `cloud_type`. Lit by a five-step march toward the
  sun (Beer-Lambert, the powder darkening on the sunlit side, a two-lobed
  Henyey-Greenstein for the silver lining and the backscatter, two
  weaker octaves for the light scattered on through them, so a low sun
  lights them from inside), the sky's zenith and horizon for their
  ambient, faded into the air with distance. Above them a sheet of
  cirrus at nine kilometres. The wind carries both, the cirrus faster.
  All of it is drawn in Godot's QUARTER-RESOLUTION sky pass, which on
  the lo-fi picture is a few thousand pixels; the GL compatibility
  renderer has no such pass, and there (EnviroSky sees which renderer
  it is) they are drawn in the main pass with three quarters the steps.
  The noise is tools/earth/make_sky_noise.py's (Texture3D, sliced).

  THE CLOCK, THE SUN AND THE MOON: an hour, a day of the year and a
  year, held still or run at `day_minutes` real minutes a day. The sun
  and the moon are placed where they really are for the place's own
  latitude and longitude (an EarthField's region.json), by Paul
  Schlyter's low-precision formulae (the sun to a hundredth of a degree,
  the moon with its biggest perturbations to about one), so OESCHINENSEE
  at 10:30 on the 15th of July has the sun 54 degrees up in the
  south-east and a moon a day past the new moon of 14 July 2026, and its
  sunset comes down behind its own ridges. The moon is drawn with its
  face lit from where the sun really is, so its phase is the real one.
  Stars twinkle when the sun is down, where the clouds let them.

  THE WEATHER (EnviroWeather, godot/island/sky/enviro/weather/*.tres, one
  resource a weather as Enviro keeps them): CLEAR, FAIR, PARTLY CLOUDY,
  CLOUDY, OVERCAST, FOGGY, LIGHT RAIN, RAIN, STORM, LIGHT SNOW and SNOW,
  each a set of numbers (coverage, density, type, base, thickness,
  cirrus, gloom, how much sun they take off the ground, haze and its
  colour, the island fog's density and start, the light, the wind, rain,
  snow, lightning). The sky is always on its way from where it was to
  the weather asked for, over `transition_seconds` (30). The pause
  menu's WEATHER chooses: CLEAR is the island's own, OVERCAST, RAIN and
  MIST (as FOGGY) the presets. An Islands entry's "sky" (hour, day,
  weather, day_minutes) is the island's own; --sky-hour=, --sky-day=,
  --sky-weather= and --sky-minutes= override it.

  THE ISLAND'S LIGHT: the same scattering, run in GDScript five times a
  second with fewer samples, gives the sunlight reaching the ground (its
  colour through the air: white at noon, gold at seven, nothing after
  sunset), the sky's light, the bounce off the ground and the horizon's
  colour; the clouds take their share of the sun and grey the sky's.
  Those become the tod_* globals every island surface is lit by (the
  moon's light at night, and a night that is dark blue, not black), the
  fog's tint (tod_haze_tint: the fog fades the ground into the real
  horizon), the plants' and the water's tint (tod_art_tint), and the
  crowd's light (EnviroSky.world_light, which Game._island_light now
  asks for when the island has one). Two new globals, tod_fog_density
  and tod_fog_near (1 everywhere else, so golf's islands are as they
  were), carry a weather's fog into the terrain, water, plant and cliff
  shaders.

  WHAT FALLS: rain and snow round the eye (GPU particles: streaks, and
  flakes drifting with the wind), lit as dark as the light under the
  clouds. A STORM throws lightning: the whole sky flashes, the bolt
  forks down in it, and the island lights up with it for a moment.

  godot/tests/earth_test.gd checks the sky on OESCHINENSEE: the sun high
  in the south-east at 10:30 in July, the moon's phase, a storm
  covering the sky and darkening the crowd's light, the island dark at
  23:00. godot/tests/earth_shot.gd takes the --sky- options and
  --look=sun|moon.


TRIPLANAR CLIFFS (0000037)

At the user's request ("triplanar cliffs too"): a real place's cliffs
are rock seen square-on, whichever way they face, and look like the
limestone walls of the Alps rather than the ground's texture hung on
a wall.

  THE GROUND (SHADER_terrain_earth.gdshader): every layer is now
  projected along all three axes and blended by how squarely the ground
  faces each (`triplanar_sharpness`, 5), not only the steep ground from
  the side as before. Each projection's normal map is turned into the
  world by the UDN blend with the mirrored-axis fix (Ben Golus's), so a
  face looking west or north is not lit inside out; the side pictures'
  up is the world's up. A projection with next to no weight is not
  sampled, so flat ground still pays for one.

  THE CLIFFS (SHADERINC_earth_cliff.gdshaderinc, new): steep rock
  carries strata — beds of lighter and darker stone about 9 m apart,
  lying near flat and bent by folds a few hundred metres across, showing
  more in some stretches of a wall than others (`strata_strength`,
  `strata_metres`) — and the dark streaks water leaves running down a
  face, long, narrow and in patches (`streak_strength`). The broad
  variation in brightness is triplanar too: it used to be off the
  ground's plan alone, which on a wall drew it in vertical stripes. Each
  side plane's pattern is blended by its weight, never the coordinates,
  so nothing folds where a face turns from looking east to looking
  south. The rock is a lighter, greyer limestone than the warm slate it
  was (rock_tint), the scree a little cooler with it.

  THE CUT FACE round the square (MAT_earth_cliff.tres, still golf's
  SHADER_cliff_wall) wears the same: the earth pack's rock and its
  normal map, now through a smooth-filtered pair (`rock_smooth`: golf's
  quantised 128 px bakes stay nearest-filtered, a 2048 px photograph
  glittered), with a second rotated sample at a quarter of the frequency
  so the tiles never line up across a face that size, the broad
  variation, and the same beds and streaks in the same world
  coordinates, so a bed running along a cliff inside the square runs on
  round the cut face. All four are off on golf's islands. The two rock
  textures are imported compressed and mipmapped now (earth_pack.py
  writes their .import); they had been neither.

  And a black tuft gone from the meadows: the third grass of
  grass_medium_01 has curled leaf tips that draw black, and stood out
  across the turf. earth_pack.py draws its first two; 33 sprites.


THE CINEMA: GOD RAYS, THE SUN'S FLARE, THE GRADE, THE SHADOWS (0000038)

At the user's request ("make it look fucking cinematic as hell —
colorgrading, sun flare, god rays"): a real place is now shot like a
film. OESCHINENSEE has all of it; golf's islands none of it.

  THE LIGHT IN THE LENS AND THE AIR (CinemaPost, godot/island/scripts/
  world/SCRIPT_cinema_post.gd, and SHADER_cinema_post.gdshader; its
  numbers in MAT_earth_cinema.tres): one pass over the whole of the 3D
  picture, after everything else in it, reading the picture and its
  depth, ADDING light (the water is drawn see-through after the copy the
  pass reads, so painting the picture back would rub it out):
    - GOD RAYS: from each pixel a march toward the sun on the screen
      (after Mitchell, GPU Gems 3), adding up the open sky near the sun
      it crosses by that sky's own colour, so a ridge, a tree or a cloud
      between cuts the light into shafts, and they take the sun's gold
      when it is low.
    - THE SUN'S FLARE: the glow and a six-bladed starburst, the long
      blue anamorphic streak across the frame, a halo with its colours
      split, and six ghosts of the iris down the line from the sun
      through the middle of the frame and past it — all as much as the
      sun can be seen (the sky and its brightness at a dozen points over
      its disc), so they go as it drops behind a peak and dim in cloud,
      and fade as it leaves the frame.
    - BLOOM off the picture's own mips, over a threshold.
    - THE AIR LIT FROM BEHIND: the haze between the eye and the far
      ground, by distance, scattered forward by the same Mie lobe the
      sky uses, so a valley seen into the evening sun goes soft and gold.
  The sky is told from the ground by its depth: the far plane, which in
  this Godot is 0 under both renderers (reversed depth).

  THE GRADE (lofi.gdshader, Lofi.set_grade; an Islands entry's "grade"):
  the picture filter grades the frame before its dither and snap: a
  film's response (Hable's curve, so the sky round the sun rolls off to
  white instead of clipping), the white balance (warmth, tint), an
  S-curve of contrast, saturation and vibrance, split toning (the
  shadows pushed toward teal and the highlights toward amber), a lens's
  vignette, and film grain. Nothing, as before, on an island that asks
  for none.

  THE SUN'S SHADOWS (EarthSun, SCRIPT_earth_sun.gd, and
  SHADER_earth_sun_bake.gdshader): the region's own heights march toward
  the sun on the GPU, a 4 m grid over the square, with a penumbra that
  widens with the distance to the ridge that casts it; baked again
  whenever the sun has moved a quarter of a degree. And the clouds'
  shadows: from each point up the ray toward the sun to EnviroSky's
  cloud layer, reading the sky's own weather map and cloud noise there,
  so the shadow on the ground is the cloud's over it and crosses the
  valley with the wind (SHADERINC_earth_sun.gdshaderinc; EnviroSky
  publishes its layer, wind and shade as globals). The ground keeps only
  the sky's light where the sun is cut off (and its highlight goes), the
  water loses its glint, the plants their share of the sun.

  The island's chunks' skirts now hang into the hill along its normal
  instead of straight down (EarthField's chunk_skirt_shaded): seen from
  below, looking up a mountainside, a crack between a fine chunk and a
  coarse one runs into the hill, and the far side showed through every
  seam as a row of lit dots.

  OESCHINENSEE now starts on an evening (Islands "sky": 18:45 on the
  15th of July, fair weather): the sun about 14 degrees up in the west
  over the Kandersteg side, the pasture and the woods on the west
  shore already in the ridge's shadow, the cliffs at the lake's far end
  in the gold. And its grade (Islands "grade"): the film's shoulder,
  a little warm, more contrast, vibrance, the split toning at 0.6, a
  vignette, grain.

  godot/tests/earth_test.gd checks the pass is there, the shadows are
  baked and on and bake again when the sun moves, and the grade reaches
  the picture (and leaves with its grain on an island without one).
  godot/tests/earth_shot.gd takes --look-off=DEG,P (the sun off the
  middle of the frame), --cine=NAME:VALUE,... (the pass's numbers, or
  off) and --noglow.


THE GAME, GONE: THE TERRAIN ENGINE ALONE (0000039)

At the user's request ("lol is there still MEWD gameplay stuff? i just
wanted the terrain engine, you can strip all gameplay elements", then
"delete gameplay code"):

  WHAT WENT: the player and the crowd, every weapon and its effects (fire,
  frost, the bore, the lance, rockets, the arc, the potato cannon, plasma,
  gore, decals, the thermal and scope sights), the drop pod, pickups,
  vehicles, the unicorns, network play and the dedicated server, the
  title, the pause menu, the HUD, the terminal, the debug and perf
  overlays, the pad wizard and touch controls, music and sound, the logs,
  the maze and its arena, CANDY LAND (its roads, houses, lamps and girls
  were the game's), DEBUG LAND, and the assets, tools, tests and plans
  (HISTORY.txt, SIGHT.txt, TOWN.txt) only they used. The fire on the
  plants (VegBurn, the billboards' burn stage and every sprite's burn map)
  and the golf wall and thermal hooks left in the island's shaders went
  with them. What stayed was found by following every reference out of
  what was kept: two thousand files went; about four hundred stayed.

  WHAT IS LEFT: the island system (godot/island/), the real places and
  their tools, the sky, the cinema, the picture filter, and a viewer
  (godot/scripts/main.gd) — the place loaded into the picture's world,
  its own sky and grade taken from its Islands entry, a camera
  (viewer/fly_camera.gd) flown with the mouse and keys, a pad or a touch
  screen, or walked over the ground (G), the hour ([ ]), the clock (T),
  the weather (F), the cinema (K), the retro picture (P), the next place
  (N), the words (viewer/hud.gd) and pictures (F12, --shot=; --y= puts the
  eye at a height in the world, under a lake's surface say). The picture
  is NATIVE now, the screen's own resolution with only the grade on it;
  the old 360 rows of dithered pixels are a key away. EnviroSky takes its
  island's "sky" from the viewer (`options`) rather than looking for a
  game, and `light_level()` is all it says about the light. The two places
  are OESCHINENSEE (the first) and NOT PENNSYLVANIA; tools/earth_fetch.py
  --register writes a new one's entry without the crowd's keys.

  THE PLANTS, AS LIGHT AS THEY CAN BE, AND SIMPLY LIT (at the user's
  request: "make sure veg only accepts ambient light, doesnt cast shadows,
  maybe even make it use an approximated bake so vegetation stays as
  lightweight as possible", then "maybe let's make the veg simple lit no
  shadows?"): SHADER_veg_billboard.gdshader is a fraction of what it was —
  the billboard, the wind, the retro texel grid, the near and far fades
  and the fog — and its light is its own, off the same tod_* globals as
  the ground: the sky's, and the sun's on a plant drawn as if it were
  round (a normal out of the card turned toward its sides and up toward
  its top, so the side toward the sun is lit and the far side has the sky
  alone), and the sun through the leaves when the eye looks toward it, so
  grass against a low sun glows. No shadow maps: no plant casts a shadow
  and none receives one; where a place has its mountains' and clouds'
  shadows baked (EarthSun), the sun's share is read once at each plant's
  middle, so a meadow in a mountain's evening shadow stays in it. Under
  that, a light BAKED into each plant where the scatter puts it (VegLight:
  a third off under the densest canopy, a little off on a steep slope,
  each plant a little lighter or darker than the next), carried in the
  instance's custom data and read with one multiply.

  THE GRASS, AS THICK AS IT GOES ROUND YOU (at the user's request: "max
  out grass thickness near player"): a second grass layer on a real place
  (GrassNear), a tuft every 25 cm out to 21 m round the eye — nearly
  thirty thousand — over the ordinary one (60 cm out to 80 m), both at
  full density and the near one's share of the field's grass cover times
  four (GrassScatter `boost`: every cell with any real turf grows a tuft;
  rock and paths stay bare), fading out by its edge
  (MAT_earth_grass_near.tres) so the carpet ends without a line. THE
  CARPET KEEPS UP: a tuft's whole cost was the field's sample under it,
  sixteen a square metre at 25 cm, so a tile of grass now asks the field
  on a coarser lattice (GrassScatter `sample_cells`: every metre near,
  every 2.4 m far) and each tuft takes its nearest lattice point's ground
  and the lattice's height under it — the near layer is down five times
  sooner, and the viewer holds the picture back until the grass round the
  eye is (`filled`), a second or so. AND EACH TUFT A MEADOW: one of Poly
  Haven's grass tufts is a few blades on a twentieth of its card, so a
  carpet of them was mostly the ground between; tools/earth_pack.py meadow
  draws a dozen of the green ones into one card (SPRITE_earth_meadow_*,
  three of them, over a quarter of the card covered, smaller and darker to
  the back, a dandelion's leaves here and there), and both layers grow
  those first (`sprite_weights`), the single tufts and the dry ones among
  them, the dandelions rarer than they were. AND ITS FOOT ON THE GROUND: a
  tuft was sunk by the worst its card could hang over a slope — half its
  width times the gradient, up to a third of it — and 12 cm more besides,
  so on a steep meadow seen up the slope the thick bottom of every card
  was under the ground and only the tips showed, in rows. It is sunk 4 cm
  now, and the scatter gives the billboard shader the ground's gradient
  (the instance's custom data), and the shader shears the card's foot up
  and down with the ground across it, whichever way it has turned. The
  grass's mowing and its maze mask, the game's, are gone.

  THE WATER, DOUBLE-SIDED (at the user's request: "give us a really pretty
  double sided water shader too with fake caustics";
  SHADER_water_earth.gdshader, rewritten): from above, the bed through it
  — the picture behind the water, bent by three octaves of waves, and
  taken by the water's true thickness off the depth buffer, red first
  (absorption per channel, in-scattering turquoise), so the shallows are
  clear and the deep its own colour; on it, the world mirrored by
  screen-space reflections (the reflected ray marched through the depth
  buffer, refined at the hit, off a surface calmer than the one the light
  goes through — `reflect_waves`, a third of the waves — so the cliffs
  stand in it nearly as in a mirror) and, where the ray leaves the picture,
  the picture's own sky; Schlick's Fresnel; a pin-sharp glint and a broad
  sheen only where the sun reaches; foam on the shore; the edge faded into
  the bank. From below, Snell's window — the world above inside 48.6
  degrees of straight up, bent and tinted — total internal reflection
  outside it, and a bright rim between. With the eye under a surface
  (SCRIPT_earth_dress.gd watches; tod_underwater), SHADER_underwater
  fogs everything by how far it is seen through the water, to the water's
  colour, darker the deeper the eye. THE CAUSTICS are on the bed itself
  (SHADER_terrain_earth, SHADERINC_earth_water): under a lake's surface the
  ground takes the sun the waves focus — a shifting net of light in three
  colours a little apart, after the well-known tileable water caustic
  (Shadertoy, joltz0r and Dave Hoskins), turned off the grid's axes,
  fading with the depth and only where the sun reaches. The water's grid
  is published as the global tod_water_level for it.

  THE PILLAR: a tall grey column stood over the hills west of the lake, in
  every picture looking that way. Not a game's at all: the water. Three of
  tools/earth_fetch.py's grows and shifts used np.roll, which wraps, so the
  small tarn on the square's east edge (2173 m) came round onto its west
  edge, where the ground is 1855 m, and the water's cut face there stood
  three hundred metres high. A shift that brings nothing in at the edge
  (`shift`) in their place, and OESCHINENSEE made again from the cache: its
  heights the same, 32 cells of water fewer, a new digest (so a new bake).

  LOADING: nothing of the place is drawn until it is up (the world's
  viewport paused, the words saying so): the building is paced by the
  frame, and a frame with the whole place in it took many times one
  without — the island came up in seconds rather than minutes. The
  mountains' shadows are baked again as the picture starts.

  THE GRADE, GENTLER IN THE DARK: the split toning is a tint of each
  colour's own brightness now, not a push, so it moves the shadows' hue
  without crushing a dark meadow to black, and the grade has a `lift` (the
  blacks raised a little toward grey, as film's are; OESCHINENSEE 0.04),
  and its film curve works on the light tones alone — the shoulder that
  rolls the sky round the sun off to white — since its toe sank a meadow
  in the evening shadow to black. And the picture is METERED, as a
  camera's is (Lofi, lofi_meter.gdshader): each frame the world's picture
  is read at 192 points, the middle counting most, averaged as the log of
  their brightness, into one pixel on the GPU, eased toward a frame at a
  time through a second (the eye adapting), and the exposure opened or
  stopped down toward a mid-grey key by it — part way (Islands "meter":
  key 0.4, strength 0.6, never under 0.7 or over 2.5 times), so an evening
  is still an evening, but a meadow in a mountain's shadow reads as grass.
  OESCHINENSEE's highlights roll off from 0.6 (its "knee") so the sunlit
  cliffs over that meadow do not burn out. The ground's ambient occlusion
  is gentler (0.55 from 0.8).

  THE BUILDS: the Linux release is the one file again (no server); the
  islands' bakes no longer carry the crowd's walking grid. tools/godot-
  test.sh runs two suites (TESTS).


VERTEX-LIT PLANTS (0000040)

At the user's request ("vertex only?"): the plants' light is worked out at
each card's four corners and blended across it, not at every pixel
(SHADER_veg_billboard.gdshader). The light was already of the simplest
kind — the sky's, and the sun's on a normal that runs straight across the
card as if the plant were round, and the sun through the leaves against it
— so it hardly bends between the corners, and the pixel, which a thick
meadow draws many layers deep, is now the picture times two colours
carried from them. The sun's share at the plant (the baked mountains' and
clouds' shadows) was read at the corners already.


FENRIR: THE NAME, THE BADGE, THE MOUSE, THE GROUND'S GREEN (0000041)

At the user's request ("so this needs to be called FENRIR, not MEWD, here
is some branding, mouse look does not work", and then "make sure grass etc
(not trees) matches the grass of the terrain splat it is on, for blending
/ visual consistencies sake"):

THE NAME: FENRIR — Fusion-Enabled Networked Real-time Interactive
Rendering — wherever the engine is seen or named: the window ("FENRIR —
OESCHINENSEE"), the app, its log lines, its pictures
(user://fenrir_*.png), the builds (fenrir-*.x86_64, fenrir-*.apk) and
their releases, and the notes. What a rename would break keeps the old
name: the repository (mewd-engine), the signing secrets the Android build
reads (MEWD_KEY*), the Android package's id (a new one would be another
app), and the tag the region grids open with (MEWDGRD1). The three islands
left over from the game (CANDY LAND, MAZE LAND, DEBUG LAND), which nothing
loaded, are gone.

THE BADGE: tools/brand/FENRIR_logo.svg, the user's, drawn at every size it
is shown by tools/brand_icons.gd (Godot's own SVG rasteriser, clear
outside its disc): the window's and the project's icon, the boot splash
(on black), Android's launcher icon and its adaptive foreground (inside
the mask's safe circle, on black), and the loading screen, which is now
black with the badge as big as it fits and the place and what is happening
under it, fading off as the place comes up.

THE MOUSE: the eye is in the picture's world (Lofi.world), a viewport of
its own, and the window's input never reaches into one by itself — so the
mouse never turned the eye, though the keys, which it reads straight off
the keyboard, moved it. The viewer hands it every event now
(FlyCamera.handle). A click takes the mouse unless it came from a finger
(a touchscreen laptop's mouse was never taken before, since any
touchscreen at all ruled it out), and the right button held down looks as
well, without taking the mouse.

THE LOW PLANTS IN THE GROUND'S COLOURS: the grass (both layers), the ferns
and the bushes — not the trees — take their colour toward the colour of
the ground at their foot (SHADER_veg_billboard `ground_match`, at the
corners with the light): the splat SHADER_terrain_earth paints there,
worked out the same way (SHADERINC_earth_ground: the twelve layers weighed
off the control pictures, the four tints, the broad variation in
brightness, the exposure) from each layer's average colour, which
tools/earth_pack.py means works out from its texels once
(textures/layers.json) and SCRIPT_earth_dress.gd publishes, with the rest,
as globals. Each texel of a plant takes the ground's colour at its own
brightness against its class's average (sprites/sprites.json means, set on
the material as `ground_reference`), so the plant keeps its light and
shade and wears the ground's hue — scaled channel by channel instead, a
green tuft went violet on a grey path — all the way at the foot (grass 0.9
of it, ferns 0.7, bushes 0.55) and a little over half that at the top, a
lit blade taken a third brighter than the turf's photograph from above,
gaps and shadows and all (`ground_level`; a fifth for ferns and bushes): a
tuft grows out of the turf in the turf's own green, browner on soil and
greyer on scree. Numbers rather than a texture's last mip, because the
sprites carry no mips and a renderer may or may not decode a colour
texture read through a global. The ground takes its four tints off the
same globals, as plain numbers, which neither renderer touches on their
way in (a colour uniform is decoded to linear by one and not the other),
so the ground and its plants agree and the turf is the same under both —
the green of these notes' pictures.

MEWD — THE GODOT PORT
=====================

(MEWD: Made Entirely Without Doom.)

At the user's request: the whole game rewritten in Godot 4 (4.3), in
GDScript, on the branch claude/godot-rewrite. The web build in js/ stays
where it is and keeps working; it is the reference every file here was
ported from, and the ported files say which JS file they came from in
their first lines.


RUNNING IT

  The project is the repository's root: open the folder in Godot 4.3
  and press F5, or from a shell:

    godot                      # the editor
    godot --path . -- --play   # straight into the game, past the title

  The web build's folders (js/, vendor/, tools/, css/, art/) carry a
  .gdignore, so Godot sees assets/ and godot/ and nothing else. The
  game's own assets are res://assets/ exactly as the web build has them.

  Arguments after `--`:
    --play               skip the title
    --seed=N             the maze for seed N (the same maze the web
                         build makes for ?seed=N — the generator is a
                         straight port, random-number call for call)
    --map=NAME           maze (the default), jesse, sprawl (THE SPRAWL,
                         the web build's ?sprawl), grid (?grid) or layers
                         (THE ANNEXE: a building in blocks, three layers)
    --at=X,Y,DEG[,LAYER] stand the start somewhere else (for pictures);
                         LAYER: on that layer's floor, on a map in layers
    --eye=RISE[,PITCH]   the eye that far over the body, tilted PITCH
                         degrees (for pictures)
    --weapon=NAME        start holding FLAMER, EXTINGUISHER, BORE,
                         MINIGUN, LANCE, LAUNCHER, ARC or POTATO
    --autofire           hold the trigger (for pictures and tests)
    --shot=out.png       render --shot-frames=N frames (default 20),
                         save the picture, quit — how every picture of
                         the port was checked
    --title              with --shot, stay on the title
    --pause              open the pause menu
    --touch              the phone's controls, without a touch screen
    --zoom=N             the scope of the gun in hand starts at step N
                         (the lance's screen, the launcher's thermal)
    --prof               print where a frame's time goes, every 2 s
                         (fps, draw calls, and the game's own sections);
                         FRAME RATE in the pause menu's DEBUG tab shows
                         the same under the frame rate, on any machine
    --edit               straight into MEWD Editor, the map editor (the
                         web build's ?edit); at the terminal, E or EDIT
    --edit-ops=FILE      with --edit, run a script of edits (the format
                         of godot/tests/editor_ops.json) — for pictures

  Controls are the web build's: WASD or the arrows (Q/E strafe too),
  mouse to look (click to capture it), Shift to run, Space to jump, F
  to use, left mouse or Ctrl to fire, Z, C or the right button to put
  the lance's scope or the launcher's thermal sight up and step its zoom
  (AIM and ZOOM on the phone), 1-7 or the wheel for the guns,
  Esc or P to pause. On the title, up/down and Enter, or the mouse.
  F2 in the game opens the map editor on the map last edited (a test
  run from the editor has an "◀ EDITOR F2" button too).

  SLOW MOTION (the Godot build's own, at the user's request): T, Y on
  the pad, or SLO on the glass, toggles it. The world runs one tic in
  four (Game.SLOW_MO) while your own body takes all four, so you walk,
  turn and fire as fast as ever through a world that crawls — bullet
  time, not a replay; the music slows with it, and the HUD says so at
  the top of the screen. Not in a network match, whose worlds must
  agree. The weapons test checks the tic count and the player's draw.

  BRAINS (game/trophies.gd; at the user's request, "let the player
  collect body parts extracted with the cerebral bore"): when a bore
  finishes its drilling and the head goes, what it was after drops out
  — a brain, out of the gore strip, which falls to the floor and lies
  there turning until a player walks over it and takes it. A trophy
  and nothing more: BRAINS n on the HUD under the gun's name
  (Player.brains), a toast as each is taken. The weapons test finds
  the one its bore kill left and walks over it.


HOW IT IS BUILT, AND WHERE EACH PIECE OF THE WEB BUILD WENT

  THE PICTURE (js/lofi.js -> godot/scripts/render/lofi.gd,
  godot/shaders/lofi.gdshader). The world is drawn into a SubViewport
  as many pixels across as the chunky PIXELS grid has columns (320 rows
  of 2:3 pixels; RENDER in the pause menu can ask for more), and the
  filter runs ONCE PER CHUNKY PIXEL into a grid-sized buffer — it
  Bayer-dithers one step and snaps to the earth palette through the
  same 32x32x32 atlas the web build builds (godot/data/palette_lut.png)
  — which is blown up to the window nearest-neighbour: a 4K window costs
  what a small one does. Every texture is sampled nearest, every
  surface is unlit (its light is worked out in its own shader), and the
  renderer is Compatibility on every platform. The gun is a second
  SubViewport in a world of its own, composited over the room and under
  the filter, exactly as the web build's overlay pass. The HUD, the
  title's menu and the pause menu are CanvasLayers above all of that and
  are never filtered.

  THE LIGHT (js/material.js -> godot/shaders/world_light.gdshaderinc).
  One set of GLOBAL shader uniforms — sky_light, light_falloff,
  min_light, global_light, air_near, air_far, air_color — declared in
  project.godot and written by the weather (godot/scripts/game/
  weather.gd), read by every surface: walls and floors, sprites, decals,
  particles. worldBand is ported line for line: depth diminishing, the
  sky's lift, Doom's 32 light steps, then the air.

  THE LEVEL (js/level.js, js/mapgeo.js, js/editor/doc.js compileDoc ->
  godot/scripts/level/). A document (the maps write one) is compiled to
  a Level of sectors and lines with a 128-unit blockmap; the collision,
  sight and hitscan are the web build's 2.5D ones — slide along the
  lines, step 24, never a physics engine — so it moves like the web
  build and like Doom. The compiler (doc_compile.gd) is compileCore:
  edges split at T-junctions, sectors inside sectors bridged in as
  holes (rooms sharing a wall one hole), free-standing linedefs stood
  up as walls, the line overrides (blocking, blind, a skin per face,
  offsets, scale, a fill in an opening), the outside wall where a roofed
  room meets the open air, no sky walls and a roof over every roofed
  room, Doom 64's sector colours and each sector's fog, the free boxes
  (props — drawn, and solid to nothing, as on the web), the plants, and
  the scatters (scatter.gd, dart for dart the web build's). MapGeo
  builds the floors (earcut.gd round holes), ceilings, roofs, walls
  whose textures follow the world (v is height, u the way round the
  room's ring: no slots, no pegs, no aligning — at the user's request),
  the middles standing in openings and the boxes,
  one mesh per texture; a level with colours, fog or two-faced walls is
  drawn with godot/shaders/world_tint.gdshader (the colour and fog per
  vertex, faces culled).

  ROOM OVER ROOM (js/level.js's COLUMNS, js/editor/doc.js's LAYERS). A
  map drawn in the editor's layers is compiled WHOLE, as the web build
  compiles it: each layer's linedefs stood up on their own, every
  layer's outlines laid over each other (DocCompile.overlay — cut where
  they cross, walked face by face), and each face built as a COLUMN,
  Level.add_column: the room of every layer over it, bottom-up, each
  storey's ceiling brought down to the underside of the DECK under the
  one over it. A deck is Level.DECK (16) thick, always — the web
  build's have none: the slab between is solid to movers, rays, eyes
  and fire, and its edge is drawn as a band (in a building's outside
  wall where the line is one); an upper floor with no room for a deck
  over the floor under it is left out and said. The room under has the
  upper floor's texture for its ceiling. A ROOF is as thick, over the
  ceiling of the room it roofs, and its edge is drawn wherever what is
  beside it is lower (MapGeo._roof_edge) — on every map with roofs.

  A BUILDING'S OUTSIDE WALL is DocCompile.WALL_THICK (16) thick, always
  (DocCompile.thick_walls; the web build's are a face with no depth):
  a roofed room drawn against the open air is its INSIDE, and the wall
  grows out of it — a strip out of each edge and a mitred corner out of
  each outward corner, each cut to the open-air sector it stands in and
  off every room, solid sector and wall already there. A wall is solid
  from the ground to the room's ceiling, the room's roof over it; on an
  upper layer, from the room's floor (__base) over whatever is under.
  A doorway (a line marked an opening) is a PASSAGE through the wall in
  the room's own floor and roof. Where a wall meets the edge of the open
  air it stands in, the open air is cut back round it. Overrides on the
  room's lines follow: the room's side (its middle becomes the wall's
  bottom, the face it sees) and the open air's side onto the wall's
  outer face. The tests that hold a build to the web build's turn it
  off (DocCompile.thick) for that comparison. Every other sector is a
  column of one — every sector of every map not in layers — and for
  those nothing below is asked or costs anything different (the level's
  `layered` is false and each new question is behind it or behind a
  column's `above`/a line's `multi`).
    - A point is in one column: sector_at answers with its GROUND storey
      (the web build's sectorAt). The storey is asked by height:
      span_in / span_at (the one whose floor..ceiling holds z, or the
      highest under it — the web build's spanIn), and stand_in for a
      body on its feet: the highest floor a step (24) or less over them,
      so the top of a stair a step under an upper floor walks onto it.
    - A line joins two whole columns (front_col, back_col). Walking
      through it asks the two storeys at the mover's height (a hall's
      door is a door, the landing over it a wall); the crowd will not
      walk off a drop of more than 96 to the LOWER floor; nobody goes
      under a deck head first. It is drawn as its BANDS — every interval
      of z where exactly one side is open, in the skin of the storey
      that is shut there — and a middle in each HOLE, where both are.
    - A building's outside wall (a roofed room against open air) is
      walled storey by storey: only in the openings where that is so
      (mid_z — a terrace beside an office is open), each storey in its
      own room's walls; a line marked as a doorway (opening) is one on
      ITS OWN LAYER only, so a ground-floor door is not a hole in the
      wall upstairs.
    - Rays: sight_blocked and ray_hit_wall ask the storeys at the
      crossing's height, and on a layered map ray_hit_flat walks the
      segment column by column for the first floor, deck or roof it
      passes through (a sky ceiling is air). So the hitscan, the eye's
      trace and sight stop at a floor, and a round can pass over an
      upper storey's edge or under it.
    - Everything that moves or lands asks the storey by height: the
      player (stand_in on his feet, span_in in the air), the crowd and
      the troops (spawned on their layer's floor, kept on their storey,
      solid only to what is at their height), the flame, frost, water,
      bore, missiles, arc, beam, embers, giblets, rain (onto the top of
      a column) and the decals under them; a blast does not reach
      through a floor. The fire grid's planes are the storeys (as in
      js/fire.js) and ignite/douse/heat_at take the height, so a fire on
      a terrace burns up there and does not come down, and one under it
      does not go up (open air under a deck has no way up; an indoor
      room climbs slowly into the storey over it, the web build's
      CEIL_SPREAD). A client puts each body in the storey at the height
      the host's snapshot gives (the wire is unchanged); sound falls off
      with height too.
    - Vehicles, the wood, the street lamps, the scatters, a match's
      spawn pads and the plants stay on the ground (as in the web build).

  THE MAPS (js/maps/ -> godot/scripts/maps/). THE MAZE, JESSE, THE
  SPRAWL (sprawl.gd) and THE GRID (grid.gd) are ported and produce the
  SAME document as the JS — JESSE was diffed against js/maps/jesse.js
  for 48 seeds (godot/tests/jesse_dump.gd); THE SPRAWL's document and
  its compiled level (every sector, line, band, side, thing, plant and
  box) against compileDoc(sprawlDoc()) (godot/tests/sprawl_dump.gd,
  which the suite runs to hold the counts), and the grid's crowd
  against buildGrid. THE ANNEXE (godot/scripts/maps/layers.gd,
  --map=layers) is the port's own: a yard, a shop with an office over
  it and a terrace beside that over open yard, drawn in two layers, with
  stairs cut by the editor's step generator — the map room over room is
  tested and pictured on.

  THE SIMULATION runs at 35 tics a second, at most six a frame, and
  the picture is drawn between the last two (godot/scripts/game/
  game.gd). The actors are the web build's state tables and actions
  (states.gd, actor.gd): Doom's chase for the troops; the crowd's watch,
  flee and contagious panic; burning, freezing, thawing, shattering,
  burning away, the bore's hold. THE FIRE THAT SPREAD IS GONE, at the
  user's request: no fuel grid, no burn meter, no haze, no wood catching,
  no brigade. People and cars still catch and burn (Actor.ignite,
  Effects.body_fire) — that is the fire the game keeps.

  THE DATA the port reads from the web build's own tables is written by
  `node tools/godot-data.mjs`: the texture list (godot/data/
  texpack.json), the palette and its LUT, and the lamp art. Everything
  else — the effect atlases, the ash — is baked in GDScript at
  start-up, from the same generators.

NETWORK PLAY (js/net/ -> godot/scripts/net/)

  The web build's LAN play, ported file for file, and ON THE SAME WIRE:
  the same seventeen-byte TicCmd in a binary WebSocket frame, the same
  JSON handshake, snapshots, pings and goodbyes in text frames, at
  ws://host:port/net. So a Godot client can join the Node host, a web
  page can join the Godot host, and either kind of client can play the
  other on either host.

    godot --headless --path . -- --server[=PORT]      host (default 7777)
          [--map=jesse|maze] [--seed=N] [--max=16] [--frags=N]
    godot --path . -- --join=HOST[:PORT]               join a host
          [--name=NAME]
    node tools/server.mjs --map maze --port 7777       the web build's host
    http://…/?join=HOST:PORT                           a web page joining

  or JOIN / JOIN host:port at the terminal (a failed join comes back to
  it saying why). JESSE is team deathmatch (A in SWAT black, B in army
  green), THE MAZE deathmatch with its crowd left at home; the network
  loadout is the minigun, and the match's rules (frag limit, respawns,
  spawn guard, the pvp scale, infinite ammo) are js/net/match.js's.
  TAB shows the table; the score line is at the top of the screen.

    net/ticcmd.gd     js/net/ticcmd.js — the command; rounded with
                      JavaScript's Math.round, so the bytes are the JS's
    net/protocol.gd   js/net/protocol.js
    net/transport.gd  js/net/transport.js + tools/server.mjs's socket:
                      a loopback pipe, WebSocketPeer, and a TCPServer
                      listener handing each upgraded line to the host
    net/session.gd    js/net/session.js (Local, Host)
    net/match.gd      js/net/match.js (NetMatch: DM/TDM, spawns, frags)
    net/server.gd     js/net/server.js (SimServer: handshake, the
                      rewind, snapshots)
    net/client.gd     js/net/client.js (NetClient)
    net/remote.gd     js/net/remote.js (NetGame: prediction and
                      reconciliation, the puppets drawn in the past,
                      toasts, the score board — drawn by render/hud.gd)
    net/host.gd       tools/server.mjs (the dedicated server's loop)

  The Game is many-player the way js/game.js is: game.players, each
  with a session (the game's own LocalSession for the one on this
  machine), game.rules (a NetMatch on a host), game.rewind, targets and
  collision among players, a match's own idea of a death. On a client
  the look taken each frame is held (game.net_look) and rounded into the
  tic's command, so the host turns you exactly as your screen did.

  Test hooks: --netbot (turn toward the nearest of the others through
  the command, walk at them, fire when in sight; --netbot=look holds
  fire), --quit-after=S, --report=PATH (a client's numbers), and on the
  host --spawns=x,y;x,y… (a deathmatch's pads) and --score-file=PATH.


A MAP IN BLOCKS (godot/scripts/level/block_compile.gd; at the user's
request: "layer 0 is an infinite plane the player could hypothetically
walk on forever; everything else is built on top of that ... treat it
all as geometric extrusion with texturing instead of how doom treats
stuff")

  THE EDITOR'S MAPS ARE NOT DOOM'S. The world is an infinite GROUND
  PLANE (a texture and the light of the open air), and a map is what is
  pulled up out of it: BLOCKS — a ring of vertices, a HEIGHT, and three
  textures, its SIDES, its TOP (what you walk on) and, where it floats,
  its UNDERSIDE (a ceiling) — and the light of the space under it. No
  room has a floor and a ceiling of its own; there are no upper and
  lower textures (a face is one picture from a block's base to its top,
  round every corner); there is no void. A building is wall blocks and
  a slab over them; a step is a low block; a pit is a block pushed DOWN
  (a negative height) into what it stands on. A block drawn inside
  another stands on it; a block on a higher LAYER stands on whatever the
  layers under it put there (the highest top under its footprint, so a
  slab over uneven ground floats over the low parts); a block given a
  BASE floats at it, and the air under it is a room. The document
  ("mewd-blocks": ground, vertices, blocks, lines, things, scatters,
  textures, world, layers) is EdDoc's; the old editor's files (rooms
  with floors and ceilings) are refused, and say why.

  HOW IT IS BUILT (BlockCompile.compile): every layer's rings, and the
  ground (a square GROUND_EXTENT = 65536 each way, as the outermost ring
  of layer 0), laid over each other (DocCompile.overlay); over each
  face of the plan the blocks round it, bottom-up (layer by layer, and
  on one layer the outermost first), raise the floor — each one's solid
  from its base to its top, an open STOREY wherever a base is over the
  floor it had, a storey of no height at every join of solid on solid
  (so each block's edge is a band of its own, in its own sides), and
  one more over the last top, under the sky (SKY_H = 8192). Those are
  Level.add_column's storeys, and the Level does the rest as for any
  map in layers: the solid between two storeys is a deck as thick as
  the block, its edge drawn in the block's sides (assign_bands: a
  band's texture is the storey over it's lower texture, which is the
  block's side), a floating block's underside the ceiling of the storey
  under it (lit by the block's light), its top the floor of the one
  over. Each storey knows its block (Sector.block, .over, .layer).
  Things and doors stand on the storey their layer leaves (stand_in:
  the highest floor among those a block of their layer or under left).
  The built area (Level.bounds_hint) is what the blockmap, the forest
  and the plants keep to; the ground runs on past it. Line overrides on
  an edge: `tex` (a skin on every face of the edge), `midTex` (a fill
  across it: a fence, a window), offsets and scale, blocking, and
  `door` {h, tex, style, auto, locked} — the slab across the edge from
  the floor to the ceiling of the storeys either side on its layer,
  swinging into the side that is a room (not the passage under the
  lintel block the edge belongs to), the lintel block's thickness its
  wall. The maze, JESSE, THE SPRAWL and THE GRID the game plays are
  still their generators' maps in rooms, compiled as they always were;
  only THE ANNEXE is a map in blocks now (godot/scripts/maps/layers.gd:
  a shop of a floor patch and four wall blocks with a lintel over its
  door, seven stair blocks, the office's floor a slab on the shop's
  walls with the office's walls on it and a terrace floating beside it,
  a roof slab on the layer over), and the layers test plays it.


THE MAP EDITOR (godot/scripts/editor/)

  MEWD Editor, in the game: MAP EDITOR on the title (whose "Back to
  the title" comes back to it), the terminal's E or EDIT, `-- --edit`,
  or F2 from the game. An editor in the mould of SLADE and Ultimate
  Doom Builder — the plan (view2d.gd), the 3D view drawn by the game's
  own MapGeo (view3d.gd), the combined workspace (3D big and the plan
  inset, Tab swaps them; Split, 2D, 3D), the top bar, the tabs down the
  left (textures, things, scatter, the map), the inspector down the
  right, the status line and info bar; Q for visual mode; undo and
  redo; the keyboard (the Help menu) — but ON A WORLD OF BLOCKS:

    DRAWING    drag on the bare ground (Blocks mode, S) or drag a shape
               out (R: rectangle, ellipse, circle, polygon, star…) or
               click the corners out (D), and the shape stands up as
               high as the toolbar's PULL (a block drawn on a block
               stands on it, in its textures); below zero it is pushed
               down into what it is drawn on. The wheel over a block in
               3D raises it (Alt: its base), PgUp/PgDn too (Shift: the
               base); Ctrl+wheel its light (over bare ground, the
               daylight). The plan shades blocks by their top's height
               (K: by brightness, by height).
    THE ROOM   with the toolbar's Make set to ROOM, a drawn footprint
               becomes a building: a floor patch inside, a wall block
               WALL_T = 16 thick along each edge as high as the Pull,
               and a roof slab over the whole footprint on the layer
               above, standing on the walls, its underside the ceiling.
    DOORS      Doors mode (O): click the side of a wall block, in the
               plan or in 3D, and a doorway is cut through it there —
               the block cut across into three, the piece under the
               click the tool's width lifted to a LINTEL floating at
               the door's height, the door on its edge; the next click
               puts in another; Shift+click takes a door out (the
               doorway stays). The inspector's panel on top sets the
               width, height, swinging or sliding, by itself or on the
               use key, the texture.
    THE SIDES  a texture clicked with a block selected is its Sides;
               Alt+click a side (3D) or a line (plan), or double-click a
               line, or the inspector's "Select its sides", selects
               every side of the block and paints them all; a plain
               click on one side in 3D paints that face alone (the
               line's skin, which covers the block's sides there). The
               inspector: Height, Base (on what is under, or floating
               at a height), the light under it, Top, Sides, Underside
               (when it floats); a line's skin, a fill across it, its
               offsets and scale, its door; the ground's texture and
               daylight in the Map tab, or click the ground in 3D.
    STEPS      the inspector's step generator (ed_steps.gd): a block
               between two heights cut across into strips, each a step
               higher (Stairs); or into rings, each standing in the
               last, up to a mound or down to a caldera (Rings).
    LAYERS     Alt+PgUp / Alt+PgDn, as before: the storeys. A block on
               layer 1 stands on the top of what the layers under put
               there; a click on a block of another layer in 3D goes to
               that layer. The other layers are ghosted on the plan.
    PICKING    the 3D view picks against the built level — the storeys'
               floors and ceilings, the lines' bands — so what you
               click is what is drawn, read back to its block, the
               ground or its line.
    GONE       floors and ceilings, inside and outside, upper and lower
               textures, Doom 64's colours and fog per room, free props
               and linedefs (a block does both), texture alignment.
               UI scale, the scatters, the texture editor, copy and
               paste, PLAY (F5, from where the 3D camera stands) and F2
               back are as they were.

    ed_doc.gd       the document: ground, blocks, lines, things, undo,
                    the tidy after an edit, problems, layers, files
    ed_ops.gd       the edits on it (a vertex splitting an edge, a
                    block cut out of the one it is drawn in, splits,
                    merges, moves, the shapes)
    editor.gd       the state, the edits a person makes (add_block,
                    add_room, place_door, nudge_height, loop_select,
                    apply_texture...), files, keyboard
    ed_ui.gd, ed_panels.gd, ed_style.gd   the bars, panels and look
    view2d.gd, view3d.gd                  the two views
    ed_steps.gd, ed_scatter.gd            the steps, the scatters
    ed_texcompose.gd, ed_texeditor.gd     the map's own textures
    ed_script.gd    a script of edits, for pictures (--edit-ops)

  UI SCALE (at the user's request): View > UI scale, or Ctrl+= and
  Ctrl+- to step it (75% to 250%), Ctrl+0 back to 100%: the whole

THE IRISH POTATO CANNON (game/potatoes.gd; the Godot build's own)

  Slot 8, at the user's request: "it will shoot potatoes that bounce
  around until they hit something, with a thick rainbow particle trail.
  when they hit something the explosion will be a spectacular color
  cycling nuke equivalent". The model is the user's
  (assets/models/potato_cannon.glb — its textures brought down to 512 on
  the long side, 24 MB to 1.6, and the barrel's knotwork UVs, which the
  file had collapsed to streaks, laid round it again), held the way the
  file draws it — the sight at the back by the eye, the hopper of
  potatoes (its glass kept glass) out front — its little screen
  a THERMAL SIGHT in night-vision green (ThermalScope with `green`: the
  launcher's heat picture run up a green ramp, a plain cross with lob
  ticks, the range in metres, six potato pips, the zoom; Z raises the
  gun so the screen is straight ahead, the hold solved from where the
  screen is in the model), and every
  other part in its OWN material — chrome, gold, red lacquer, the
  knotwork's normal maps, the shamrock decal — lit by a rig of its own
  (weapon3d "pbr": a key, a fill, a made-up sky for the metal to
  reflect) instead of the flat gun shader the other guns wear. A potato
  leaves down the sight, lofted, falls, and BOUNCES off walls, floors
  and ceilings until it touches somebody — or its eight-second fuse
  runs out, or it rolls to a stop; the shooter is safe from it for the
  first few tics. Behind it a thick trail of added light, a hue on each
  puff. The warhead: a nuke's worth of blast and fire, a white flash,
  and a fireball, a climbing column, a cap and a ring of shock, every
  colour and cycling (shaders/nuke.gdshader), a storm of rainbow sparks
  and the heaviest shake in the game. While a potato is in the air the
  palette gives way by half, and while a nuke burns all but completely
  (Lofi.set_unsnap), so the rainbow reaches the glass as a rainbow. Six
  in the hopper, one back every two seconds. godot/tests/potato_shot.gd
  takes its pictures; the weapons test fires it.

THE WEB BUILD (the site)

  The GitHub Pages site is this, exported for the web: Godot 4.3's Web
  export (export_presets.cfg, "Web"), the Compatibility renderer on
  WebGL 2, and no threads — GitHub Pages cannot send the headers a
  browser wants before it lends a page threads, so the export is the
  no-threads one, and the editor compiles in place instead of on its
  thread when OS.has_feature("threads") says there are none.
  tools/build-web.sh [outdir] makes it (Godot 4.3 on the path and its
  web export template installed); the publish job in
  .github/workflows/pages.yml fetches both (the template kept in the
  Actions cache), exports it to the root of the site, and puts the web
  build in js/ (tools/build-site.sh) under classic/. Hosting a match
  needs a machine (--server); a page can JOIN one over WebSocket.

WHAT IS NOT PORTED YET

  - Of the map compiler: the storeys and slopes of a SECTOR (its own
    `storeys` and `floorSlope` — off in the web build too, FEATURES in
    js/editor/doc.js; a map in LAYERS is the way to build upstairs, and
    is built); fog FOLLOWED along the line of sight from sector
    to sector (a surface here is fogged by its own sector's over the
    whole distance), and a sector's thing colour and fog on sprites and
    plants.
  - Small picture differences left after the lighting pass: sprites
    and the sky get the map's default fog, not the fog of the sectors
    along the line of sight; the web's smoke on world surfaces; the
    grid sky's palette snap order; JESSE's lamp-post colour and red
    barrels; a few extra rooftop conifers in THE SPRAWL.
  - Of MEWD Editor: see THE MAP EDITOR above — the Godot-scene export.
  - Of room over room: the web build's decks and outside walls have no
    thickness and these always do (not a port, a change); the crowd's
    scare and the rain's spawning ask the plan; a match's spawn pads are
    on the ground.
  - Of network play: the Node host's serving of the web build's files
    on its own port (the Godot host is a socket and nothing else; serve
    the page from anywhere), the `use` key in the command (the Godot
    player has no use yet), and the web page's saved player name (a
    Godot client is --name=NAME or a random PLAYER nnn).

NOT TO BE PORTED (the user's decision): the VTOL gunship, breaches and
ruins, and rebuilding the geometry of charred sectors.

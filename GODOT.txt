MEWD — THE GODOT PORT
=====================

At the user's request: the whole game rewritten in Godot 4 (4.3), in
GDScript, on the branch claude/godot-rewrite. The web build in js/ stays
where it is and keeps working; it is the reference every file here was
ported from, and the ported files say which JS file they came from in
their first lines.


RUNNING IT

  The project is the repository's root: open the folder in Godot 4.3
  and press F5, or from a shell:

    godot                      # the editor
    godot --path . -- --play   # straight into the game, past the title

  The web build's folders (js/, vendor/, tools/, css/, art/) carry a
  .gdignore, so Godot sees assets/ and godot/ and nothing else. The
  game's own assets are res://assets/ exactly as the web build has them.

  Arguments after `--`:
    --play               skip the title
    --seed=N             the maze for seed N (the same maze the web
                         build makes for ?seed=N — the generator is a
                         straight port, random-number call for call)
    --map=NAME           maze (the default), jesse, sprawl (THE SPRAWL,
                         the web build's ?sprawl) or grid (?grid)
    --at=X,Y,DEG         stand the start somewhere else (for pictures)
    --weapon=NAME        start holding FLAMER, EXTINGUISHER, BORE,
                         MINIGUN, LANCE, LAUNCHER or ARC
    --autofire           hold the trigger (for pictures and tests)
    --shot=out.png       render --shot-frames=N frames (default 20),
                         save the picture, quit — how every picture of
                         the port was checked
    --title              with --shot, stay on the title
    --pause              open the pause menu
    --touch              the phone's controls, without a touch screen
    --zoom=N             the scope of the gun in hand starts at step N
                         (the lance's screen, the launcher's thermal)

  Controls are the web build's: WASD or the arrows (Q/E strafe too),
  mouse to look (click to capture it), Shift to run, Space to jump, F
  to use, left mouse or Ctrl to fire, Z, C or the right button to put
  the lance's scope or the launcher's thermal sight up and step its zoom
  (AIM and ZOOM on the phone), 1-7 or the wheel for the guns,
  Esc or P to pause. On the title, up/down and Enter, or the mouse.


HOW IT IS BUILT, AND WHERE EACH PIECE OF THE WEB BUILD WENT

  THE PICTURE (js/lofi.js -> godot/scripts/render/lofi.gd,
  godot/shaders/lofi.gdshader). The world is drawn into a SubViewport
  at RENDER rows (960), and one full-screen pass averages it onto the
  chunky PIXELS grid (320 rows of 2:3 pixels), Bayer-dithers it one step
  and snaps it to the earth palette through the same 32x32x32 atlas the
  web build builds (godot/data/palette_lut.png). The gun is a second
  SubViewport in a world of its own, composited over the room and under
  the filter, exactly as the web build's overlay pass. The HUD, the
  title's menu and the pause menu are CanvasLayers above all of that and
  are never filtered.

  THE LIGHT (js/material.js -> godot/shaders/world_light.gdshaderinc).
  One set of GLOBAL shader uniforms — sky_light, light_falloff,
  min_light, global_light, air_near, air_far, air_color — declared in
  project.godot and written by the weather (godot/scripts/game/
  weather.gd), read by every surface: walls and floors, sprites, decals,
  particles. worldBand is ported line for line: depth diminishing, the
  sky's lift, Doom's 32 light steps, then the air.

  THE LEVEL (js/level.js, js/mapgeo.js, js/editor/doc.js compileDoc ->
  godot/scripts/level/). A document (the maps write one) is compiled to
  a Level of sectors and lines with a 128-unit blockmap; the collision,
  sight and hitscan are the web build's 2.5D ones — slide along the
  lines, step 24, never a physics engine — so it moves like the web
  build and like Doom. The compiler (doc_compile.gd) is compileCore:
  edges split at T-junctions, sectors inside sectors bridged in as
  holes (rooms sharing a wall one hole), free-standing linedefs stood
  up as walls, the line overrides (blocking, blind, textures, offsets,
  scale, pegging, a face per side), the outside wall where a roofed
  room meets the open air, no sky walls and a roof over every roofed
  room, Doom 64's sector colours and each sector's fog, the free boxes
  (props — drawn, and solid to nothing, as on the web), the plants, and
  the scatters (scatter.gd, dart for dart the web build's). MapGeo
  builds the floors (earcut.gd round holes), ceilings, roofs, walls
  with Doom's pegging, the middles standing in openings and the boxes,
  one mesh per texture; a level with colours, fog or two-faced walls is
  drawn with godot/shaders/world_tint.gdshader (the colour and fog per
  vertex, faces culled).

  THE MAPS (js/maps/ -> godot/scripts/maps/). THE MAZE, JESSE, THE
  SPRAWL (sprawl.gd) and THE GRID (grid.gd) are ported and produce the
  SAME document as the JS — JESSE was diffed against js/maps/jesse.js
  for 48 seeds (godot/tests/jesse_dump.gd); THE SPRAWL's document and
  its compiled level (every sector, line, band, side, thing, plant and
  box) against compileDoc(sprawlDoc()) (godot/tests/sprawl_dump.gd,
  which the suite runs to hold the counts), and the grid's crowd
  against buildGrid.

  THE SIMULATION runs at 35 tics a second, at most six a frame, and
  the picture is drawn between the last two (godot/scripts/game/
  game.gd). The actors are the web build's state tables and actions
  (states.gd, actor.gd): Doom's chase for the troops; the crowd's watch,
  flee and contagious panic; burning, freezing, thawing, shattering,
  burning away, the bore's hold. The fire grid (fire.gd) is identical to
  js/fire.js tic for tic on the three scenarios in godot/tests/fire_*.

  THE DATA the port reads from the web build's own tables is written by
  `node tools/godot-data.mjs`: the texture list (godot/data/
  texpack.json), the palette and its LUT, and the lamp art. Everything
  else — the fire art, the effect atlases, the ash — is baked in
  GDScript at start-up, from the same generators.

TESTS

    tools/godot-test.sh

  runs the headless suites: the weapons against real people in the
  maze (godot/tests/weapons_test.gd — minigun, flamer, extinguisher,
  bore, launcher, arc), the fire grid, the forest, and the JESSE
  generator, and THE SPRAWL and the grid against the web build's counts.
  A shell with xvfb can also render pictures with --shot.


WHAT IS NOT PORTED YET

  - Of the map compiler: LAYERS (a map in storeys) and the storeys and
    slopes of a sector (off in the web build too, FEATURES in
    js/editor/doc.js); fog FOLLOWED along the line of sight from sector
    to sector (a surface here is fogged by its own sector's over the
    whole distance), and a sector's thing colour and fog on sprites and
    plants.
  - Small picture differences left after the lighting pass: sprites
    and the sky get the map's default fog, not the fog of the sectors
    along the line of sight; the web's smoke on world surfaces; the
    grid sky's palette snap order; JESSE's lamp-post colour and red
    barrels; a few extra rooftop conifers in THE SPRAWL.
  - THE MEWD EDITOR and NETWORK PLAY — mission critical, being ported
    now. The editor is an in-game runtime editor (godot/scripts/editor/)
    reading and writing the web build's own document JSON. Network play
    keeps the web build's wire protocol, so a Godot client can join the
    Node server (tools/server.mjs) and a headless Godot server
    (--server) can host web and Godot clients alike.

NOT TO BE PORTED (the user's decision): the VTOL gunship, breaches and
ruins, and rebuilding the geometry of charred sectors.

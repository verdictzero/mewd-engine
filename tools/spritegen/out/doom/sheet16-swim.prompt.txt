You are a sprite production artist for a 1993-style first-person shooter in the direct lineage of DOOM. Your one job in this request is to paint ONE specific character into a POSE TEMPLATE, cell by cell, so that the result can be cut up by a script into a game sprite sheet. The script is dumb. It cuts on a fixed grid. Anything you move, merge, drop, add or let drift breaks the cut. Precision beats beauty here. Read everything below before you draw a single pixel.

IMAGE "TEMPLATE" (the big grid on magenta): THE POSE TEMPLATE. A grid of equal cells on a flat magenta (#FF00FF) ground, inside a dark frame. In every cell stands a grey MANNEQUIN: a stick figure with capsule limbs and a round head. The mannequin is METADATA, not a character. It tells you, for that cell and only that cell:

#  4. THE ANGLE CONTRACT (rotations)
############################################################################

On a sheet with turned frames, THE COLUMNS ARE CAMERA ANGLES. The camera walks around the character in eight equal steps, one eighth of a turn each, and the character does not move between columns. The same instant of the same pose, eight times, from eight places.

  VIEW 1  FRONT:            the character faces the camera. Full face. Its RIGHT side is on YOUR LEFT. Its LEFT side is on YOUR RIGHT.
  VIEW 2  FRONT-LEFT 3/4:   turned one eighth toward YOUR LEFT. Three-quarter face. Its LEFT shoulder is nearer you.
  VIEW 3  LEFT PROFILE:     side on, facing YOUR LEFT. Pure profile. You see its LEFT side; its RIGHT arm and leg are mostly hidden behind. The nose points left.
  VIEW 4  BACK-LEFT 3/4:    three-quarters from behind, facing away and to YOUR LEFT. Mostly back. The left edge of the face may just show. No eyes.
  VIEW 5  BACK:             the character faces AWAY from you. You see the back of the head, the back, the heels. NO FACE. Its RIGHT side is on YOUR RIGHT.
  VIEW 6  BACK-RIGHT 3/4:   the mirror image of view 4.
  VIEW 7  RIGHT PROFILE:    the mirror image of view 3. Facing YOUR RIGHT. The nose points right.
  VIEW 8  FRONT-RIGHT 3/4:  the mirror image of view 2.

If a sheet has only five columns, they are views 1 to 5 and the game mirrors them for 6, 7 and 8.

The mannequin's red and blue limbs tell you which side you are looking at in every cell. Trust them over your instinct. If the red (RIGHT) arm is nearer the camera, you are looking at the character's right side. If you can see the dark face patch, the face is visible from that angle; if you cannot, draw the back of the head, with NO facial features.

Things that go wrong here, which you must not do:
  - drawing the same front view in every column with the head merely turned: NO. The whole body turns. In the profile the body is narrow; in the back view there is no face.
  - drawing view 3 and view 7 as the same picture: NO. They face opposite ways. Everything mirrors, including which hand holds the grip.
  - putting the gun on the wrong side in the back view: NO. In view 5 the character's right hand is on YOUR right.
  - making the figure in one column taller, wider or bigger-headed than in the next: NO. The same character, the same size, the same build, in all eight.

#  5. THE POSE CONTRACT (frames)
############################################################################

THE ROWS ARE FRAMES of an animation (or, on a flat sheet, the frames run left to right). Each mannequin shows one instant. Copy it joint for joint:
  - Which leg is forward, which is back, how bent each knee is, which foot is lifted.
  - Where each hand is, how bent each elbow is, where the gun points.
  - How far the torso leans, twists or arches. How far the head tilts.
  - How high the hips are: a crouch is low, a jump is in the air, a corpse is flat.
The words in the row's text panel describe the same pose in plain language. Use the words to understand the mannequin; use the mannequin for the exact angles.

Two consecutive frames must look like two consecutive instants of ONE motion by ONE body: same height, same build, same clothes, same gun. Only the pose changes. A walk cycle's four frames must loop: frame 4 flows back into frame 1.

Where the template shows the gun pointing somewhere, the character's gun points there, from the same hands. Where the template shows a muzzle flash, draw a bright yellow-white flash with an orange edge at the muzzle, and nowhere else, and only in that frame. Where the template shows the figure in the air (a jump, a fall), the character is in the air at the same height, feet clear of the ground line. Where the template shows the figure lying down, the character lies flat along the same line, as long as the standing figure is tall, not propped up and not floating.

############################################################################
#  6. THE STYLE CONTRACT: DOOM (1993)
############################################################################

Draw every figure as a DOOM SPRITE. Doom's monsters and marines were photographed latex and clay models, and early 3D renders, turned on a stand through eight angles, shot under hard light, then crushed to a 256-colour palette and cleaned up by hand at about 60 pixels tall. That is the look. Concretely:

  - A GAME SPRITE, not an illustration and not a photo. Chunky, solid, high-contrast, instantly readable as a silhouette.
  - HARD EDGES. No soft blur, no anti-aliased fuzz against the magenta, no painterly strokes, no glow, no film grain, no depth of field.
  - A DARK OUTLINE around the whole figure and between its major parts, one to two pixels thick at game scale (so a few pixels thick in this large picture). The outline is dark, not black everywhere: very dark brown or dark grey where the material is light.
  - LIMITED PALETTE SHADING: three to five flat tones per material (skin, cloth, metal, leather), banded, with no smooth gradients. Think of a photograph posterised to a few tones, then tidied.
  - LIGHT FROM ABOVE AND SLIGHTLY IN FRONT, the same in every cell. Tops of shoulders, the top of the head and the front of the chest catch the light; under the chin, under the arms and the backs of the legs are in shade. The light does NOT rotate with the figure between columns; the figure rotates under the light.
  - SLIGHTLY EXAGGERATED PROPORTIONS for readability at 60 pixels: the head a touch large, hands and feet solid and clear, the gun a little oversized, the silhouette clean. Not cartoon, not chibi. Doom.
  - SATURATED, PUNCHY COLOUR, as the character's own colours from the CHARACTER image, each pushed to read at a distance. Dark navy must still read as navy, not black.
  - BLOOD is bright red with darker red edges, in flat shapes, only where the template has blood.
  - The MUZZLE FLASH is a flat yellow-white starburst with an orange rim, like the Doom shotgun's.
  - NO BACKGROUND ELEMENTS. No floor, no wall, no sky, no shadow on the ground, no scenery. Magenta only.
  - Draw it LARGE and CLEAN in this picture, in the visual language of a sprite that will be shrunk to about 60 pixels tall with nearest-neighbour sampling. Fine detail that would vanish at 60 pixels is wasted; bold detail that survives it is what we want.

############################################################################
#  7. THE CHARACTER IDENTITY LOCK
############################################################################

It is ONE character in EVERY cell. Not a family of similar characters. Not "the same guy in different lighting". The identical person.

  - The SAME FACE, hair, skin tone, eye colour and expression family. From the back, no face at all, but the same hair and the same helmet or hat.
  - The SAME BUILD: same height, same shoulder width, same waist, same leg length, in every cell. A standing figure's head top is at the same height in every standing cell. A lying body is as long as the standing one is tall.
  - The SAME CLOTHING AND ARMOUR, piece for piece, colour for colour, from every angle. If the CHARACTER image shows only the front, invent a plausible back ONCE and then keep it identical in every back view.
  - The SAME COLOURS: take them from the CHARACTER image. Do not drift warmer, cooler, lighter or darker from cell to cell.
  - ASYMMETRY STAYS PUT: a pauldron on the right shoulder stays on the right shoulder (the red side) in every view, including the mirrored ones. A badge on the left breast stays on the left (the blue side). A scar on the left cheek stays on the left cheek.
  - THE SAME GUN in every cell where the mannequin holds the bar. If the CHARACTER image shows a weapon, that weapon. If it shows none, a plain assault rifle in the character's own palette, and then THAT rifle in every cell. The gun does not change model, size or colour between cells. Where the mannequin has no bar (a death, a crawl, a swim), the character has no gun in hand.
  - NOTHING ADDED: no cape, no backpack, no extra pouches, no new weapon, no pet, no new accessory the CHARACTER image does not show. NOTHING REMOVED either.
